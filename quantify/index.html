The Approach

How we put a number on structural strain.

Partners make decisions in financial terms. Structural problems arrive as a stalled decision, a frustrated administrator, or a good associate who leaves. Quantify is the step that translates one into the other.

What we count

Four places the strain shows up in partnership economics.

Partner time

Hours partners spend on operational work and decisions that could sit elsewhere with clear decision rights.

Decision delay

How long important decisions wait, and what the waiting costs in missed work, rework, and slower response to clients.

Unchosen turnover

People the firm wanted to keep who left for reasons the structure contributed to, and the cost of replacing them.

Growth the structure cannot absorb

Work, clients, or lateral hires the firm turns away or loses because the current model cannot carry them.

How we build it

Conservative ranges, with every assumption written down.

The inputs come from interviews across the firm, with partners, other professionals, and staff, and from the firm's own documents, set against patterns from peer firms. Figures the firm gives us are checked before they go into a report.

The result is a range. The low end uses assumptions a skeptical partner would accept, and each assumption is listed so the partners can challenge it and adjust it themselves.

In the Firm Evaluation, the estimate gives the firm a defensible starting number. In Firm Architecture, we test it against the firm's financial records and refine it.

How we frame it

A measure of what the firm could be reaching.

Structural strain is usually the product of success. The firm won the work, hired the people, and opened the offices, and the structure fell behind. The number describes capacity the firm has already earned and is not yet using.

That framing shapes what happens next. Partners who see the figure that way start planning how to reach it.

An illustration

What the arithmetic looks like.

Every figure below is invented for illustration. Take a firm of 120 people with 20 partners, working 46 weeks a year.

Source of strainAssumptionLowHigh
Partner time on work that could sit elsewhere2 to 4 hours a week per partner, valued at $300 to $450 an hour, with only half of the recovered time redeployed to client work or business development$276,000$828,000
Unchosen turnover2 to 4 departures a year the firm wanted to avoid, at a replacement cost of $60,000 to $120,000 each$120,000$480,000
Annual capacity not yet realized$396,000$1,308,000

Partner time: 20 partners × 2 hours × 46 weeks × $300 × 50% = $276,000 at the low end, and 20 × 4 × 46 × $450 × 50% = $828,000 at the high end. Decision delay and unabsorbed growth are left out of this illustration, which keeps it conservative.

A real Evaluation builds each line from what the firm's own people describe, and the partners see every input.
