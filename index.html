How we think

The model that built your firm may be holding it back.

As firms grow, their structure tends to lag behind them. The signs are stalled decisions, partners pulled into operational problems, good people leaving, and friction between practice areas. The usual responses are a technology investment, another offsite, a chief of staff, or a new bonus pool, and the operating model underneath stays the same.

The firms that move past this point treat their own organization with the rigor they apply to client work. They take stock of what is working and what is straining, redesign the operating model around the firm they have become, and let the old version go.

Professional services firms are expert organizations that are rarely expert at being organizations.

Who we work with

Mid-sized firms that have run up against their own structure.

Our clients are law firms, accounting practices, architecture and engineering firms, and financial advisory practices of roughly 50 to 300 people, and they usually call us in one of two situations.

Growth

The firm is bigger and more complex than the structure it was built on. Decisions that used to happen in a hallway now wait for weeks.

Friction

The practice side and the operational side are each capable, and they are pulling in different directions.

The first call usually comes from a COO, director of operations, or executive director, the person who sees the structure most clearly. The decision to go ahead belongs to the managing partner, and often the partnership.

How we work

The High Peaks Group Approach

Every engagement follows the same four steps.

  1. Assess

    We look at how the firm runs today: where decisions stall, where practice and operations pull apart, and which structures and habits are carrying the firm and should be kept.

  2. Quantify

    We put the strain in partnership terms: partner hours spent on operations, decisions that wait, turnover the firm did not choose. The number describes what the firm could be reaching.

    How we quantify

  3. Design

    We design the structural changes with the people who will own them: the operating model, decision rights, accountability, and the order of the transition.

  4. Sustain

    We set up the governance that keeps the design in place: who owns what, which signals show drift, and what prompts a correction.
